Solution to Challenge statement.
Our solution is a social productivity app that helps users reach their goals by putting something on the line. Most productivity apps rely on self-discipline, which is easy to ignore when no one is watching. Our app however adds real accountability by having users stake an embarrassing photo of themselves on each goal. If they fail to follow through with their goal that photo is posted to their friends feed. The idea is that the pressure of not wanting friends to see the photo and know they failed their goal pushes users to complete their goals consistently.


Project Vision
For people that want to be more productive who need help managing their goals, the Stake app is an accountability platform that allows users to “stake” embarrassing photos of themselves to highly motivate users to complete their goals. Unlike Habitify, which relies on reminders that are easy to ignore, our product creates social pressure by making users' progress visible to friends and a personal stake for each goal.

User Story
To get started, a user creates a goal, such as going to the gym every day. The user then sets how often the goal must be completed, such as every day or three times a week, along with a daily deadline for checking in.
Before a goal becomes active, the user uploads an embarrassing photo of themselves as their stake. The photo must follow the app's content guidelines, which require that it shows only the user and contains no nudity, sexual content, hateful symbols, or anything dangerous or illegal. Other users and app admins can remove and ban users who break the rules. The stake stays hidden as long as the user keeps up with their goal, and it cannot be deleted while the goal is active.
To prove they completed their goal, the user uploads a photo of themselves doing it before the deadline. Each check-in is timestamped and linked to the user's account. Friends and collaborators can see each check-in and can flag any that look fake, which keeps users honest. The app also sends reminders as the deadline approaches so users have a chance to check in on time.
Every user's profile displays a calendar for each of their goals. Days with a completed check-in are marked on the calendar and show that day's photo, while missed days are marked separately. This calendar gives friends a clear picture of how much progress the user has made over time.
If a user misses a check-in within the time range they set, the app automatically posts their stake photo to the feed, where their friends can see it. After the penalty, the user can upload a new stake photo to keep working toward the goal.
The social side of the app allows users to send and accept friend requests, view their friends' goals and progress calendars, and scroll through a feed of their friends' check-ins and released stake photos. Friends can react to each other's check-ins to offer encouragement. Users can also report any content they believe breaks the guidelines.
The app has two types of users. Members create goals, upload check-ins and stake photos, and interact with their friends. Administrators review stake photos before goals begin, handle reported content, remove photos that break the guidelines, and manage user accounts.
